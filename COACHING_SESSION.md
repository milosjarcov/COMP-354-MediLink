
# MediLink Coaching and Leadership

## 1. Coaching Scenario

The MediLink development team has experienced delays in reviewing pull requests.

During the simulated coaching session, the developer discussed the challenge of balancing new development tasks with existing pull-request review responsibilities.

The engineering manager conducts a coaching conversation to help the developer understand the difficulty and identify a practical improvement.

**Session format:** Engineering coaching conducted between two team members.

**Engineering manager:** Yoanna

**Developer:** Milos

**Scenario:** Delays in pull-request reviews due to competing development responsibilities.

## 2. Coaching Conversation

Manager: What has been making it difficult to complete pull-request reviews on time?

Developer: I have been focusing on new development tasks and sometimes postpone reviews until I finish my current work.

Manager: How do you decide whether to prioritize new development or an existing review?

Developer: I usually prioritize the task assigned to me, even when another developer is waiting for feedback.

Manager: What effect do you think this has on the team's workflow?

Developer: It can delay other developers because they cannot move their work into verification or completion.

Manager: What could you change to balance these responsibilities more effectively?

Developer: I could check pending reviews before starting another development task and reserve time for reviewing existing work.

Manager: What support would help you make this change?

Developer: Clear review responsibilities and a shared expectation about review response times would help.

Manager: How could we evaluate whether the change is effective?

Developer: We could compare pull-request review turnaround times and check whether fewer work items remain waiting for review.

## 3. Lessons Learned

The coaching conversation highlighted how unclear prioritization and competing development responsibilities can contribute to delayed pull-request reviews rather than necessarily indicating a lack of individual effort.

It also demonstrates how coaching questions can help developers recognize workflow problems and propose improvements independently.

## 4. Agreed Improvement Action

During the simulated coaching session, the engineering manager and developer agreed to introduce a lightweight pull-request review prioritization practice.

The developer will check outstanding review requests before starting additional development tasks and identify reviews that may be blocking other team members.

The engineering manager will help clarify review ownership and establish reasonable response-time expectations.

The effectiveness of this action will be evaluated by monitoring pull-request review turnaround time and the number of work items waiting for review.

## 5. Evaluation Method

The proposed action could be evaluated by comparing:

- Average pull-request review turnaround time
- Number of work items waiting for review
- Developer feedback about workload and interruptions

The team should examine these measures before and after introducing the change.

## 6. Collaborative Leadership Decision

Example: Establishing a pull-request review policy.

This decision benefits from collaboration because developers understand the practical challenges of balancing implementation and review responsibilities.

Discussing the policy together allows the team to establish realistic expectations and encourages shared ownership.

## 7. Directive Leadership Decision

Example: Responding to a critical appointment-reminder incident.

During a production incident, the engineering manager may need to provide direct guidance by assigning investigation responsibilities, prioritizing corrective work, and coordinating verification.

Direct leadership is appropriate when urgent decisions are necessary to protect service reliability.

## 8. Conclusion

Effective engineering leadership requires balancing developer autonomy with clear accountability.

Coaching encourages independent problem-solving, collaborative decisions promote team ownership, and directive leadership provides clarity when urgent situations require immediate coordination.
